## How to choose a jump starter

- **Match the engine.** Use the engine sizes the manufacturer lists. Diesels need more current than petrol engines of the same size.
- **Starting current, not just peak.** Peak current is a short surge and is the larger number on the box. Starting (or cranking) current is closer to what the starter actually delivers.
- **Battery capacity.** A larger mAh rating means more jump attempts per charge and more power-bank use, but a heavier unit.
- **Safety features.** Look for reverse polarity, short-circuit and overheat protection.
- **Temperature.** Lithium batteries dislike heat. Do not leave a jump starter in a hot car for long, and charge it before summer and winter.
- **Keep it charged.** Top it up every few months so it works when you need it.

## How to use a jump starter

1. Switch off the car, lights and accessories.
2. Connect the red clamp to the positive (+) battery terminal and the black clamp to the negative (-) terminal or a bare metal part of the engine.
3. Switch on the jump starter if it has a power button, and wait for its ready indicator.
4. Start the car. If it does not start within a few seconds, wait a minute and try again.
5. Once the engine runs, switch off the jump starter, then remove the black clamp first and the red clamp second.

Follow the manual for your unit. If the battery is old or the car fails repeatedly, have it tested.
