Qubo Q600 vs Philips AC1715 is really a choice between two ideas of a purifier. The Qubo Q600 is built around raw air-moving power for a big room at a mid price. The Philips AC1715 is built around a quiet, small-footprint machine with a trusted brand, a longer warranty and a sensor that reads allergens as well as PM2.5. Both are Wi-Fi smart purifiers with True HEPA-class filters, and both cost roughly ₹15,000 to ₹17,000, so the choice comes down to room size, noise, filter cost and how you will actually use them.

This comparison uses the manufacturers' own spec pages and the live Amazon.in listings, checked on 11 Nov 2026. Where two sources disagree, we say so rather than pick the bigger number.

## Qubo Q600 vs Philips AC1715: the short answer

- **Pick the Qubo Q600** if your room is large (a hall or living room of 350 to 600 sq ft), you live in a smoggy city such as Delhi NCR, and you want the most clean air per rupee.
- **Pick the Philips AC1715** if the purifier goes in a bedroom or a room up to about 380 sq ft, you care about night-time noise, and you want a 2-year warranty from a long-established brand.
- **Skip both** if you only need a bedroom purifier on a tight budget. The non-smart [Philips AC1711](/philips-air-purifier-ac1711-63-vs-ac1715-60/) or the Coway Airmega 150 cost less or run cheaper over time (see the cost table below).

## CADR and room size: the Q600 moves 50% more air

CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate) tells you how many cubic metres of smoke-sized particles a purifier clears per hour. It is the single most useful number when comparing purifiers. Higher is better, and it is the figure that decides whether a purifier keeps up with a room.

The Q600's listing gives 450 m³/h. The Philips AC1715 is rated at 300 m³/h by Philips itself. On paper that is a 50% advantage for Qubo. A common rule is to multiply the room's volume in cubic metres by about 5 to get the CADR you need for roughly five air changes per hour, which is what you want for polluted cities.

Worked example, a 12 ft x 15 ft bedroom with a 10 ft ceiling: that is 1,800 cubic feet, or about 51 cubic metres. 51 x 5 is roughly 255 m³/h. Either purifier clears that comfortably. Now take a 20 ft x 18 ft hall with a 10 ft ceiling: 3,600 cubic feet, about 102 cubic metres, which wants roughly 510 m³/h for five changes an hour. The Q600 gets closer, the AC1715 falls well short. For a hall like that, consider the [Philips AC3220](/best-air-purifier-for-delhi-ncr/) or two purifiers.

Be careful with the "up to 600 sq ft" claim. Coverage figures are marketing numbers based on a low number of air changes per hour. For real winter smog, treat the Q600 as a 350 to 450 sq ft purifier and the AC1715 as a 250 to 300 sq ft purifier. For sizing in detail, see our [best air purifier for Delhi NCR guide](/best-air-purifier-for-delhi-ncr/).

## Noise: the AC1715 is the better bedroom machine

Philips publishes a range of 15 dB in sleep mode to 50 dB at full speed. A reading near 15 dB is quieter than a whisper and below what most people notice. Qubo does not publish decibel figures on the pages we checked, and moving 450 m³/h generally takes more fan speed, so expect the Q600 to be louder when it runs hard. If light sleepers share the room, the Philips has the edge. Test the Q600 on its sleep mode before you commit, and keep it a metre away from the bed.

## Filters: what you actually pay each year

The purifier's price is only part of the cost. The filter is a recurring bill.

- **Philips AC1715** uses the FY1700/30 filter. Philips lists it at ₹4,995, and recommends replacing it about once a year. A third-party "compatible" FY1700 was listed on Amazon at ₹3,339 with a 3.4 rating from 12 buyers, which is thin evidence. We would stay with the genuine one. Details are in our [FY1700 filter guide](/philips-ac1711-filter-philips-ac1715-filter-complete-guide/).
- **Qubo Q600**: Qubo says the filter should be replaced once a year and that filters are sold through its store. We could not find a published filter price on the pages we checked, so ask the seller before buying. The listings quote 9,000 hours on Amazon and 15,000 hours elsewhere. Neither will last that long in heavy smog, so plan for annual change.

Over three years with two filter changes, the Philips works out at about ₹16,599 + ₹9,990 = ₹26,589. We cannot give the Qubo figure without a price for its filter, so check that number before deciding.

## Smart features and sensors

Both pair with an app, schedule runs, show live air quality and respond to Alexa or Google Assistant. The Philips AeraSense sensor shows PM2.5 and an allergen index on a coloured ring plus a number, which is easy to read from across a room. The Qubo shows a live PM2.5 value. Both need a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi network, so a router that only broadcasts 5 GHz will give you setup trouble. Both also work manually without Wi-Fi, which matters if the app is ever down.

## Build and use: small differences that matter

- **Footprint:** the Philips is a compact tower for a bedside or corner. The Q600 is larger, so allow clear space on all sides, since a purifier jammed against a wall loses airflow.
- **Cleaning:** wipe the pre-filter and sensor every two to four weeks in dusty areas. Qubo's own guidance is every 15 to 30 days.
- **Placement:** keep either unit at least 20 to 30 cm from walls and away from curtains.
- **Gases:** neither is a dedicated formaldehyde machine. If cooking fumes or paint smells are your concern, check the carbon layer. Philips lists active carbon. Qubo does not clearly describe its carbon layer, so do not buy the Q600 specifically for odours.

## Which one should you buy?

**Buy the Qubo Q600 if:**
- The room is over about 350 sq ft.
- You are in Delhi NCR, Lucknow, Patna or another city with heavy winter smog, and the extra CADR matters.

**Buy the Philips AC1715 if:**
- It goes in a bedroom or a room under about 300 sq ft.
- Noise and sleep matter, or you want allergen readings.
