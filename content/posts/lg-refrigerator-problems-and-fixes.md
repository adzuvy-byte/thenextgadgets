LG refrigerators have a strong reputation for design, energy efficiency, and innovative features like the InstaView panel and craft ice maker. But owning one long enough usually means running into at least one of the well-documented issues that LG owners discuss repeatedly online, from compressor failures to ice makers that stop working for reasons that have nothing to do with the hardware. This guide covers every common LG refrigerator problems, what's actually causing it, and what you can do about it, including what's realistic to fix yourself and when you're better off calling a technician.

## Problem 1: LG Refrigerator Not Cooling (Fridge Warm, Freezer Fine)

This is the most common complaint for LG refrigerators, and the symptom is specific: the fridge compartment feels warm and food starts spoiling, but the freezer still seems to be working normally. This distinction matters because it points to a different cause than a complete cooling failure.

**The most likely cause: evaporator fan or air damper failure.** This almost always means the evaporator fan has failed or the air damper is stuck closed. Cold air is being produced in the freezer but can't reach the fridge compartment. The evaporator fan circulates cold air from the freezer into the refrigerator section through a damper or air duct. If the fan motor burns out or the damper gets stuck shut, the freezer keeps working fine because it has direct access to the cooling coil, while the fridge section gets no cold air at all.

**Other causes to rule out first:**

- Check if the vents between the freezer and fridge compartments are blocked by food items pushed against them
- If you recently had a power outage or surge, your fridge might have reverted to Demo Mode and will not cool. Press and hold the Refrigerator and Ice Plus buttons for 5 seconds to restore normal function. The display will show actual temperature instead of "off."
- Dirty condenser coils on the back or underneath the unit restrict airflow and reduce cooling efficiency over time. Vacuuming them once or twice a year is straightforward preventive maintenance.

**Fix:** Resetting Demo Mode and clearing blocked vents are DIY steps. Replacing an evaporator fan motor is a moderate DIY repair if you're comfortable with basic disassembly. A technician can typically diagnose and fix this in a single visit.

Ask your authorised service centre for a written quote for the fan motor, including parts and labour, before you agree to the repair.

## Problem 2: LG Refrigerator Not Cooling At All (Both Freezer and Fridge Warm)

When both compartments are warm and the lights are still on, this is a different and more serious situation than the above.

**The most likely cause: failed compressor or start relay.** If both the freezer and fridge compartments are warm but the lights are on, your compressor or start relay is likely the culprit. The compressor is the core of the entire cooling system, and when it fails, refrigerant stops circulating completely.

You may hear a clicking sound as it tries to start, or complete silence. Other causes include a dirty condenser coil or a faulty start relay, which is a small part that is often misdiagnosed as a full compressor failure.

**Check the start relay first before assuming compressor failure.** The start relay is a small component attached to the side of the compressor that helps it start. It is inexpensive and a straightforward replacement. Remove it and shake it near your ear. If it rattles, it's failed and needs replacing. This simple check can save you an unnecessary compressor replacement.

**Check your compressor warranty.** LG usually gives a longer warranty on the compressor than on the rest of the fridge. Check the terms for your model on the warranty card before paying for any compressor work.

If your fridge is within the compressor warranty period, contact LG before paying for any compressor work, as parts may be covered even if labour is not.

If your fridge is more than eight years old and facing a compressor replacement, compare the written quote with the price of a new unit before committing to the repair.

## Problem 3: LG Ice Maker Not Working

Ice maker malfunctions are one of the two most widely reported and concerning issues with LG refrigerators. The good news is that the majority of ice maker failures are caused by simple, fixable issues rather than hardware failure, and there's a logical order to check them.

**Step 1: Confirm the ice maker is actually turned on.** Many LG refrigerators have a small on/off toggle located on the ice maker unit itself. Ensure it hasn't been accidentally bumped into the Off position. This sounds too simple, but it's genuinely the cause more often than you'd expect.

**Step 2: Check freezer temperature.** According to LG's official troubleshooting guidance, the ice maker shuts off production at 10 degrees F or warmer. The freezer must be set to -4°F (-20°C) or colder for reliable ice production. If your freezer temperature is borderline, fix the cooling issue first and the ice maker will often resolve itself.

**Step 3: Reset the ice maker.** Unplug the refrigerator or turn the breaker off for at least 30 seconds. Then locate the small test button on the bottom or side of the ice maker assembly. Press and hold for 3 seconds. The ice tray should rotate and dump during the test cycle. If it completes the test cycle successfully but still doesn't produce ice on its own, the issue is likely a component failure rather than a software error.

**Step 4: Check for a frozen fill tube.** A frozen fill tube is a very common cause of ice maker failure that looks like a hardware fault but isn't. Use a hair dryer on a low, cool setting to melt the ice around the fill tube and the tray. This often restores ice production immediately.

**Step 5: Check the water inlet valve.** Most LG refrigerators use a dual-valve system. The secondary door valve fails while the primary valve continues to supply the water dispenser. This is why you might have drinking water but no ice. Testing each solenoid for continuity with a multimeter confirms whether a valve has failed. Replacing the secondary valve is a moderate DIY repair with parts available widely online.

**Step 6: Check the water filter.** LG refrigerators use an internal water filter to remove contaminants. A clogged or overdue filter restricts water flow enough to stop ice production. LG recommends replacing the filter every six months. If yours is overdue, this is the first thing to try before any other step. A new OEM or compatible filter costs around $30 to $50.

Get a written quote before an ice maker repair, since the cost depends on which component has failed.

## Problem 4: LG Refrigerator Making Noise

LG refrigerators are generally quiet compared to many competitors, so unusual noises stand out when they happen. The type of noise almost always points to the specific cause.

**Clicking sound when trying to start:** This points directly to a start relay or compressor issue, as described in Problem 2 above. The clicking is the compressor attempting and failing to start repeatedly.

**Loud humming or buzzing:** A continuous, loud hum is often caused by a failing condenser fan motor or an ice maker that can't complete its cycle. Check whether the sound correlates with the ice maker attempting to cycle.

**Rattling or vibrating:** Usually the refrigerator isn't level and is vibrating against the floor or a cabinet. Adjust the leveling feet at the bottom front corners until the fridge sits firmly without rocking.

**Gurgling or hissing:** This is actually normal. Some sounds are simply the refrigerant moving through the cooling system and don't indicate a problem. Gurgling sounds during or after a cooling cycle are expected.

**Loud cracking or popping:** Thermal expansion sounds when the interior walls flex slightly as temperature changes. Normal, especially in newer units.

**What's actually concerning:** Persistent clicking, a complete change in the usual operating sound level, or a grinding noise from the back of the unit behind the lower panel. Any of these warrant investigation rather than waiting to see if they resolve.

## Problem 5: LG Refrigerator Water Dispenser Not Working

According to some refrigerator repair professionals, water and ice dispenser issues are common with LG fridges. LG has essentially acknowledged this by maintaining a dedicated troubleshooting page for water and ice dispensers on its own website.

**Check in this order:**

- Confirm the water supply line behind the fridge is fully open and not kinked
- Check whether the water filter is due for replacement, a severely clogged filter will slow or stop dispenser flow before affecting anything else
- If the water dispenser is producing a slow trickle rather than nothing at all, low water pressure from the household supply line is the most likely cause. LG specifies a minimum of 20 psi for proper dispenser function
- If the dispenser makes a humming sound when you press the paddle but no water comes out, the water inlet valve is suspect
- If the dispenser is completely silent when you press it, check whether the child lock feature is activated (usually a button combination listed in the manual)

## Problem 6: Frost and Ice Buildup in Freezer

For LG bottom freezer refrigerators, a common complaint is excessive frost buildup caused by a faulty defrost system. When frost accumulates heavily in the freezer rather than being cleared automatically by the defrost cycle, this indicates the defrost heater, defrost thermostat, or defrost timer has failed.

**DIY check:** Unplug the fridge and let the freezer fully defrost manually for 24 to 48 hours. If cooling returns to normal after this, you've confirmed the defrost system is failing since the problem will simply recur. A manual defrost is a temporary fix, not a permanent one.

**The actual repair:** A defrost heater or thermostat replacement is a moderate DIY job if you're comfortable disassembling the freezer interior panels. If the defrost system failure has been left unaddressed long enough to ice over the evaporator coils completely, a technician visit is more practical since thorough defrosting before component access is time-consuming.

## Problem 7: Door Gasket Tearing or Not Sealing

An LG French door refrigerator gasket tearing from the bottom upward after approximately 18 months is a real, reported issue on multiple units. A failing door gasket allows warm air to enter the fridge continuously, which makes the compressor work harder, increases energy consumption, and leads to moisture buildup and frost.

**Test:** Close the door on a piece of paper. If you can pull the paper out without resistance, the gasket isn't sealing properly at that point.

**Fix:** Replacement door gaskets for LG refrigerators are available from LG directly and from third-party parts suppliers. Installation is a DIY job on most models, involving pressing the new gasket into the door channel without tools. If the gasket is tearing due to a warped door rather than the gasket itself degrading, door alignment adjustment is needed first.

## Problem 8: LG Refrigerator Error Codes

LG refrigerators display error codes on the control panel when specific faults are detected. The most commonly reported ones:

**Er FF:** Freezer fan error. The freezer evaporator fan has stopped. This is the same issue described in Problem 1 above for a warm fridge.

**Er IF / Er FS:** Ice fan or ice maker sensor error. The ice maker sensor or fan has failed.

**Er DH / Er DL:** Defrost heater error. Confirms the defrost system issue described in Problem 6.

**Er CO:** Communication error between the main control board and display board. Try a full power reset (unplug for 5 minutes) first. If the code returns immediately, the control board or its wiring harness needs professional diagnosis.

**Er rS:** Refrigerator sensor error. The temperature sensor for the fresh food compartment has failed and needs replacing.

For any error code, a full power reset is always the first step. Unplug the refrigerator for at least 5 minutes before plugging it back in. If the code clears and doesn't return, it was a temporary fault. If it returns consistently, the component named by the code needs inspection.

## Quick Checks Before You Book a Technician

Test the start relay before assuming the compressor has failed, since a failed relay can mimic a compressor fault. Replace an overdue water filter before diagnosing any ice or water dispenser issue. Both are common false positives that lead to unnecessary service calls.

## When to Repair vs When to Replace

If your LG refrigerator is under 5 years old and under warranty, repair is almost always the right call. If it is 8 to 10 years old and facing a compressor replacement, compare the written quote with the price of a new unit before spending money on ageing hardware. If the compressor is still under its warranty, the parts may be covered even if you pay for labour.

Before any major repair, call LG directly and have your model and serial number ready. Even out-of-warranty units have sometimes received goodwill repair assistance for well-documented compressor issues.
