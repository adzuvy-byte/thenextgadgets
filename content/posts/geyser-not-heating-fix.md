A geyser that has stopped heating is rarely a mystery. A handful of causes account for most cases, and the first few checks need nothing more than a look and a switch. This guide is ordered from the easiest and safest to the ones that need a professional.

**Safety first.** Geysers combine mains electricity and water. Switch off at the isolator or MCB before touching anything, never open the cover on a live unit, and call a technician for any internal work.

## Quick triage

1. **Is there power?** Check the indicator light, the switch and the MCB.
2. **Is the thermostat set sensibly?** Orient Electric, a geyser maker, suggests keeping it around 50 to 60°C.
3. **Is the hot-water outlet working?** A blocked outlet or a partly closed valve can make hot water seem weak.
4. **Has heating time crept up over months?** That points to scale.
5. **Does the unit trip the MCB?** That points to an electrical fault. Stop and call a technician.

## Check 1: Power and the indicator light

If the light is off, the unit is not getting power. Look at the wall switch, the plug and the MCB. A geyser has a high load, so a loose plug or socket can heat up and fail. If you smell burning, see discoloured plastic or the MCB trips again as soon as you reset it, do not keep switching it on.

## Check 2: Thermostat setting

Many geysers have a dial. If it was turned down for summer and never turned back, the water may simply be lukewarm. Set it to a moderate temperature and give it time to heat.

## Check 3: Give it enough time

Check the capacity. As a rough guide, a 15 L geyser at about 2 kW needs around 20 to 25 minutes from cold, and a 25 L one needs around 35 to 45 minutes. In winter, with colder incoming water, add more time. If it matches the usual pattern, the geyser is probably fine.

## Check 4: Hard water scale

Scale is a mineral crust that forms on the heating element and inside the tank when the water is hard. It acts like insulation, so the element works harder and heats more slowly. The warning sign is gradual: heating that takes noticeably longer over weeks or months. Descaling means draining the tank or removing the element, so ask the manufacturer's service team to do it, especially while the unit is under warranty.

## Check 5: Mixing and flow

If hot water arrives and then turns lukewarm quickly, the tank may be small for your use, or a mixer tap may be letting cold water in. Try drawing a small amount from the geyser outlet directly to compare.

## Check 6: Heating element or thermostat fault

If power reaches the unit, the light is on and the water stays cold after a normal heating time, the element or thermostat may have failed. This is a repair for a technician. Do not test it yourself.

## Check 7: Tripping or leaking

If the MCB trips, there is water around the unit, or you see rust-coloured water, switch off at the isolator and call for service. These point to a leak or insulation fault.

## Check 8: Age of the unit

Storage geysers wear out. If yours is many years old and has needed repeated repairs, compare the repair cost against a new unit with a better BEE rating.

## When to call a technician

- The MCB trips repeatedly
- You smell burning or see sparks
- There is a leak or dampness near the unit
- The geyser is not heating despite normal power and time
- It is under warranty (do not open it yourself, since that may affect the warranty)
