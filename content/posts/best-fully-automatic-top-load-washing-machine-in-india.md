## How to choose

- **Capacity.** 6 kg for one or two people, 7 kg for three to four, 8 kg for larger families.
- **Star rating.** 5 stars save electricity over the years.
- **Inverter motor.** Quieter and more durable.
- **Water pressure.** Check the minimum pressure the machine needs.
- **Lint filter and drum.** A good filter and a steel drum help with long-term care.
- **Service.** Check the service centre near you.

## What to know before buying a top loader

- **Size by load, not by family size.** The 7 kg models suit 3 to 4 members on the listings. Wash blankets or bulky items often, and an 8 kg machine gives you more room.
- **Check your water pressure.** Fully automatic machines list a minimum water pressure. Low-pressure homes should look at near-zero-pressure designs such as the Haier.
- **Hard water changes the choice.** Hard-water features such as Aqua Energie on the IFB or Hard Water Wash on the Whirlpool reduce scale and dullness on clothes.
- **Look at the motor warranty.** The motor is the costliest part to replace. Bosch lists 12 years and Haier 10 years on the motor.
- **Energy figures are comparable.** The lower the kWh per kg, the cheaper the machine is to run. Differences are small per wash and add up over years.
- **Allow for installation.** Check that installation is free, that you have a tap and a drain point within reach, and that the base is level. A noisy, wobbling machine is usually an installation problem.

## Top load or front load?

Top loaders are cheaper, faster to load and easier on the back, and they let you add clothes mid-cycle. Front loaders are gentler on clothes and use less water but cost more. For most Indian homes with hard water and a limited budget, a good top loader is the practical choice.
