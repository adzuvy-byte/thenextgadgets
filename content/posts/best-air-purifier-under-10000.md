## What ₹10,000 buys you, and what it does not

At this price, expect:
- **A True HEPA (H13) filter and a carbon layer** on most models, which is enough for dust, pollen and fine smoke particles.
- **A CADR of 120 to 270 m³/h.** By the usual sizing rule (see our guide to [what CADR means](/what-is-cadr-in-air-purifier/)), that suits a bedroom or a small living room of roughly 150 to 300 sq ft.
- **A PM2.5 display or colour indicator** and an auto mode.

Do not expect strong gas removal. Carbon layers in this price range are thin, so a purifier here will reduce cooking smells and smoke odours only modestly. ## How to choose between them

1. **Measure your room first.** Multiply length by width in feet. As a rule of thumb from the EPA sizing chart, you want a CADR of roughly 1.1 m³/h per square foot of floor (8 ft ceiling). A 150 sq ft bedroom needs about 165 m³/h, a 250 sq ft room about 275 m³/h. Our [CADR guide](/what-is-cadr-in-air-purifier/) shows the working.
2. **Decide if you want an app.** The DREO purifiers, Qubo Q200 and Mijia have one; the Philips AC0920 and Honeywell models do not.
3. **Check filter availability.** Before you buy, search for the replacement filter and its price. A cheap purifier with a hard-to-find filter costs more in the long run.
4. **Prefer a brand with local service** if you will rely on the warranty.

## Running costs and filters

Most of these purifiers use 20 to 35 W. Running one 12 hours a day costs roughly ₹60 to ₹100 a month at ₹8 per unit. Filters need replacing about once a year, longer in clean air and shorter in smoggy cities. For reference, the genuine filter for the related Philips AC0950 costs ₹2,720 and the Coway Airmega 150 filter ₹3,299, while the Qubo Q200 filter is rated for 9,000 hours. Always check the current price of the specific filter before you buy.

## Mistakes to avoid when shopping under ₹10,000

- **Trusting the "covers 700 sq ft" headline.** Coverage depends on how many air changes per hour the maker assumed. Use CADR instead.
- **Buying an ionizer or UV model for "extra" cleaning.** The EPA warns that ionizers and some UV devices can produce ozone, and says there is no good evidence that UV or plasma devices remove gases in home units.
- **Ignoring noise.** If the purifier goes in a bedroom, look for a sleep-mode figure under about 25 dB.
- **Putting it in a corner.** Leave 20 to 30 cm clear around it.
