Xiaomi Mijia 6 vs Qubo Q600 is a curious matchup because the two machines are almost the same where it counts. Their airflow differs by 7 m³/h (443 against 450), they are both aimed at big living rooms and both come with a phone app. Yet the Mijia costs ₹17,999 and the Qubo ₹14,990. The question is whether the ₹3,009 gap buys you anything you will notice.

We went through both Amazon.in listings on 13 Nov 2026 and checked prices for genuine filters. We have not tested the machines ourselves, and where a figure is not published we say so rather than fill the gap.

## The short answer

- **Buy the Qubo Q600** if you want the most air for the least money and care mainly about particles: winter smog, dust, pollen. It is 17% cheaper and its genuine filter price is public (₹3,390).
- **Buy the Xiaomi Mijia 6** if the home has new paint, new furniture or a kitchen that fills the house with cooking fumes, or you want a published 32 dB sleep level, a colour display with five sensors and a published formaldehyde rating.
- **Neither is right for a bedroom-only purchase.** Both are big-room machines. For a bedroom, see the [Coway Airmega 150](/coway-airmega-150-vs-philips-ac1715/) or the [Mijia 4 Compact](/mijia-4-compact-vs-qubo-q200/).

## Airflow: effectively a draw

CADR is the volume of clean air a purifier delivers per hour. The Mijia 6 is rated at 443 m³/h and the Q600 at 450 m³/h, a 1.6% difference that nobody could notice in a room.

Divide each by 5 to get the volume it can clean five times an hour, the rate most people want in a polluted city, then convert for a 10 ft ceiling:

- Mijia 6: 443 ÷ 5 = 89 m³, about **313 sq ft**.
- Qubo Q600: 450 ÷ 5 = 90 m³, about **318 sq ft**.

Xiaomi's own low-end figure (312 sq ft) matches that calculation almost exactly, which is a good sign for how the company states its range. Qubo's "up to 600 sq ft" works out to about 2.6 air changes an hour. Both claims are fine for ordinary air, and for heavy winter smog (Delhi NCR in November) we would hold each of them to roughly 250 to 350 sq ft. If you need to refresh your memory on the sums, our [CADR guide](/what-is-cadr-in-air-purifier/) shows them.

Per rupee, the Q600 is clearly ahead: about ₹33 per m³/h against ₹41 for the Mijia 6.

## What the extra ₹3,009 gets you on the Mijia 6

**A formaldehyde rating.** Xiaomi publishes a formaldehyde CADR of 218 m³/h and says the purifier removes 98% of formaldehyde and VOCs. That is useful information, because very few purifiers in India publish a gas figure. Two cautions: carbon layers have limited capacity and fill up, so a purifier is a supplement to ventilation after painting or furniture delivery, not a replacement. And Qubo's absence of a number does not prove the Q600 does worse, it only means you cannot check.

**Five sensors and a colour display.** The Mijia reads PM1, PM2.5, dust and allergens, temperature and humidity, and shows them on a colour screen. The Q600 shows PM2.5 as a number on the unit. If you like looking at data, or want to see PM1 (ultra-fine particles from smoke and vehicle exhaust), the Mijia gives more.

**A published sleep noise level.** Xiaomi says 32 dB in sleep mode. Qubo does not publish a decibel figure for the Q600 on Amazon.in. Moving 450 m³/h generally takes more fan speed, so expect the Q600 to be audible when it runs hard.

**A magnetic back panel.** Xiaomi's listing highlights a magnetic door that makes the 12-month filter change easy.

**UV-C light and negative ions.** Treat these as extras, not reasons to buy. Particle cleaning depends on airflow and HEPA filtering, which are the same in both. Ion generators in particular are something some buyers prefer to avoid, and we did not find an ozone figure for the Mijia 6, so if that worries you, ask Xiaomi before you buy.

## What the Q600 does better

**Price, by a clear margin.** ₹14,990 against ₹17,999.

**A public, reasonably priced genuine filter.** Qubo lists the Q600 filter at ₹3,390, rated for 9,000 hours. At 12 hours a day that is about two years, though in smog you may want to change it sooner. Over three years, the Q600 comes to roughly ₹14,990 + ₹3,390 = ₹18,380 with one filter change. For the Mijia 6 we cannot give a figure, because we did not find a genuine filter price. That is the biggest unknown in the Xiaomi's favour or against it.

**A five-layer filter that lists activated carbon, a nano-silver coating and True HEPA H13.** On paper that matches the Mijia's stack. The Mijia lists UV-C and ions where Qubo lists extra pre-filtering and silver.

**A number on the unit itself,** not just in the app.

## Ratings and warranty

The Mijia 6 shows 4.2 out of 5 from 1,014 ratings and the Q600 4.0 from 1,421. Both figures are pooled across a brand's linked variants (the Mijia number is shared with the 4 Compact and the Qubo number with the Q200), so they do not separate the two models. Both come with a 1-year warranty. Neither brand has the service record of Philips or Coway for air purifiers, so check how a claim works in your city before you buy.

## Who should buy which

**Buy the Xiaomi Mijia 6 if:**
- You have a freshly painted or newly furnished home, or heavy cooking odours.
- You want a published formaldehyde rating, PM1 reading and a quiet sleep mode.
- You are comfortable paying about ₹3,000 more and finding the filter price from the seller.

**Buy the Qubo Q600 if:**
- You want the most airflow for the money and your concern is particles.
- You want a known genuine filter price before you commit.
- You prefer a live PM2.5 number on the unit.
