Most people ask "geyser or instant geyser?" as if one is simply the faster, cheaper version of the other. They are not. A **storage geyser** heats a large tank and keeps it hot, so you can run a full bath or shower. An **instant geyser** (also sold as a "instant water heater") heats a small tank, usually 3 litres, in a couple of minutes. It is meant for a kitchen sink or a basin, not for bathing.

Choosing wrongly is the main mistake here. Put an instant geyser in the bathroom and you will wait, then run cold. Put a storage geyser in the kitchen and you will pay for a large tank and a long wait when you only wanted warm water for dishes. This guide explains which fits where, with the numbers behind it.

## The short answer

- **Bathing, a household of 2 to 5, or any shower use:** a storage geyser, 15 L or 25 L.
- **Kitchen sink, wash basin, quick hot water for utensils or a baby bottle:** a 3 L instant geyser.
- **Both needs in one home:** it is common to fit one of each. They do different jobs and cost little to run for what they do.

## How each type works

A storage geyser holds 10 to 25 litres or more, heats it with an element, and a thermostat switches the element off when the water reaches the set temperature. Hot water is drawn from the top and cold water enters at the bottom. Because the tank is insulated but never perfect, a small amount of energy goes into keeping the water hot while you are not using it.

An instant geyser has a small tank, commonly 3 litres, with a high-power element, often 3 kW. The tank is small enough that the element brings the whole volume to temperature in a few minutes. Many models can also be switched off immediately after use, so there is little heat left waiting around.

## The numbers that decide it

Basic physics gives you a feel for the difference. Heating one litre of water by one degree takes about 4.18 kilojoules.

**Heating time**
- A 3 L instant geyser with a 3 kW element, heating water from about 25°C to 60°C, needs roughly 0.12 kWh. At 3 kW that is a little under 2.5 minutes in the ideal case, before losses.
- A 25 L storage geyser with a 2 kW element, heating the same 35°C rise, needs roughly 1 kWh. At 2 kW that is about 30 minutes in the ideal case, so expect 35 to 45 minutes in a real home.
- A 15 L storage geyser needs about 0.6 kWh for the same rise, so about 20 to 25 minutes.

**What happens when the small tank runs out**
At 3 kW, an instant geyser can add about 35°C of heat to only roughly 1.2 litres of water per minute. So the first 3 litres arrive hot. After that the output is a trickle of hot water, or lukewarm water if you open the tap fully. This is why it suits washing up and not showers.

**Rough running cost**
Heating 20 litres of water from about 22°C to 45°C for a bath takes about 0.53 kWh of electricity before any losses. At ₹8 per unit, that is roughly ₹4.30 per bath. The same energy is needed whichever geyser type supplies the water. These are estimates, not measurements. Your tariff, incoming water temperature and how long the geyser stays switched on will change them.

## Side by side

**Capacity.** Storage: 10 to 25 L and up. Instant: usually 3 L, with a few larger "rapid" models around 6 L.

**Waiting time.** Storage: 20 to 45 minutes from cold. Instant: about 2 to 4 minutes.

**Power drawn at once.** Storage: commonly about 2 kW, around 9 amps. Instant: commonly 3 kW, around 13 amps.

**Standing losses.** Storage: some, because it keeps water hot. Instant: very little, if you switch it off after use.

**Space.** Storage: a wall-mounted tank, 15 to 25 L, in the bathroom or on a balcony. Instant: a small cylinder that fits under a kitchen counter or above a basin.

**Hot water volume.** Storage: enough for one to three people to shower. Instant: a few litres, then a trickle.

## Wiring and safety, which many buyers skip

A 3 kW instant geyser draws about 13 amps (3,000 W divided by 230 V). That is a heavy load for an ordinary light-duty socket. Makers ask for a 15 or 16 amp power point, wiring of a suitable thickness, a correctly rated MCB and a working earth connection. If your kitchen wiring is old, get an electrician to check it before you install one. A weak socket can overheat.

A storage geyser at 2 kW draws about 9 amps, which is lighter, but the same rules on earthing and a proper MCB apply. Always switch off at the isolator before anyone touches a unit that has water leaking near it, and never let a child handle the controls.

## Hard water and upkeep

Both types suffer from scale if your water is hard. Scale on the heating element slows heating and makes the unit work harder. Orient Electric, a geyser manufacturer, advises checking the thermostat setting (50 to 60°C is its suggestion) and descaling when heating times have clearly lengthened over weeks or months. Descaling the tank or element means draining the unit, so if you are not comfortable with that, have it done through the manufacturer's service.

Storage tanks are also worth inspecting if the geyser is several years old. Leaks from the tank body, rust-coloured water or a persistent smell all need a technician.

## Which should you buy? A quick decision guide

**Choose a storage geyser if:**
- You want to shower or bathe from it.
- Four people shower in the morning, or you have a large family.
- You want hot water ready in the bathroom, with a thermostat that holds the temperature.

**Choose an instant geyser if:**
- You only need hot water at a kitchen sink or wash basin.
- You prefer a small unit that does not take up bathroom wall space.
- Your wiring supports a 15 or 16 amp load.

**Choose both if:** the kitchen is far from the bathroom, or you are tired of waiting for hot water for dishes.

## Buying tips that save money

- **Check the star label.** The BEE label shows energy use. If you run a geyser daily, a 5 star unit costs a little more at purchase and less to run.
- **Match size to people.** Oversized tanks cost more to buy and, being larger, lose more heat while standing.
- **Look at warranty terms.** Geysers usually carry separate warranties on the tank, the element and the unit. Compare them and read the terms.
- **Plan the install.** Ask who will mount the geyser, who supplies the pipes and valves, and whether the wall can take a full 25 L tank, which weighs over 25 kg before you add its own weight.
- **Do not skip a pressure-relief valve.** Storage geysers should be installed with the safety valve the maker supplies. Never block it.
