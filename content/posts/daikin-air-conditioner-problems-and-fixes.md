## Problem 1: Daikin Air Conditioner Not Cooling

No cooling is the most frequent complaint with Daikin air conditioner units. Before assuming a refrigerant or compressor issue, work through these causes in order since the vast majority of "not cooling" complaints resolve with one of the first three steps.

**Dirty or clogged air filter — always check this first.** A dirty filter is the most common cause of inadequate cooling. It restricts airflow and prevents the unit from absorbing heat properly. Daikin recommends cleaning the air filter every two weeks during heavy use. Remove the filter panel on the indoor unit, take out the filter, rinse under warm water, allow it to dry fully, and reinsert it. If the filter hasn't been cleaned in months, this single step often restores full cooling performance immediately.

**Incorrect mode or temperature settings.** Confirm the unit is set to Cool mode, not Fan, Dry, or Auto mode that might be running fan-only. Also confirm the set temperature is actually lower than the current room temperature. A Daikin unit will not cool if the set point is already at or above room temperature.

**Coil freezing — ice on the indoor unit.** If you can see frost or ice forming on the indoor unit or feel no airflow from it, the evaporator coil has frozen. This happens when airflow is restricted, typically from a dirty filter, but also from blocked vents or a low refrigerant charge. Switch the unit off and run it in Fan mode only for 60 to 90 minutes to defrost the coil. Then address the underlying cause before switching back to Cool mode.

**Undersized unit for the room.** If the unit runs but does not cool effectively, a common cause is that the unit is not appropriately sized for the room, unable to overcome the heat load. An undersized Daikin will run continuously at full capacity and still fail to hit the set temperature. This isn't a fault in the unit itself — it's a sizing problem that requires either upgrading the unit or reducing the heat load in the room (better insulation, window treatments, reducing heat sources).

**Refrigerant leak.** If the filter is clean, the coil isn't frozen, and the unit is properly sized but cooling has gradually declined over weeks or months, a refrigerant leak is the likely cause. Refrigerant doesn't deplete in a healthy system — low refrigerant always means a leak. Signs include the indoor unit blowing air that's cool but not cold, ice forming on the copper lines, or a hissing sound near the outdoor unit. Low refrigerant requires professional assistance — adding refrigerant without locating and fixing the leak is a temporary fix that will recur. A technician must pressure-test the system, locate the leak, repair it, and recharge correctly.

**Dirty condenser coils on the outdoor unit.** The outdoor unit dissipates heat into the environment. If the condenser coils are coated in dirt, leaves, or debris, they can't release heat effectively and cooling performance drops across the whole system. Gently rinsing the outdoor unit with a hose while powered off clears most surface buildup.

## Problem 2: Daikin Air Conditioner Not Turning On

**Check the power supply first.** Confirm the circuit breaker hasn't tripped. Daikin split systems draw significant current on startup and can trip a breaker, particularly if sharing a circuit with other high-draw appliances or if there's been a recent power surge.

**Check and replace remote control batteries.** On Daikin split systems, the remote is required for most operations. Dead batteries in the remote look exactly like a dead indoor unit. Replace before any further diagnosis.

**Check whether the unit is in timer off mode.** Daikin's weekly and daily timer settings are easy to activate accidentally. An active timer in the off window will prevent the unit from starting even though everything else is functioning normally. Press the Cancel or Timer button on the remote to clear any active timer.

**Full power reset.** If the unit won't respond to any commands, turn it off at the circuit breaker or isolator switch and leave it off for 5 full minutes before restoring power. This clears any fault state the control board may have entered after a power interruption or surge.

**Check whether the outdoor unit is running.** Sometimes the indoor unit appears to start normally but the outdoor unit doesn't activate. This indicates a communication fault, a tripped outdoor breaker, or a high-pressure protection trigger. If the outdoor unit fan isn't spinning after the indoor unit starts, this warrants a technician check.

## Problem 3: Daikin Air Conditioner Leaking Water

Water leaking or dripping is one of the most common problems reported with Daikin units.

**Blocked drain line — most common cause.** The indoor unit produces condensate as it cools air, which drains through a pipe to the outside. If this drain line gets blocked by algae, mold, dust, or debris, water backs up inside the unit and drips from the indoor head. Clear the drain line regularly to prevent blockages. Flush it by pouring a cup of diluted bleach solution through the drain access point on the indoor unit. If water is actively dripping from the unit, switch it off until the drain is cleared to prevent water damage below.

**Improper installation angle.** The indoor unit needs a very slight downward tilt toward the drain outlet so condensate flows naturally toward the pipe. If the unit was installed level or tilting the wrong way, water pools in the drain tray and eventually overflows. Correcting this requires adjusting the mounting brackets, which is a job for an installer.

**Dirty evaporator coil.** A coated coil sheds condensate unevenly, causing drips to fall forward rather than collecting in the drain tray below. Regular coil cleaning as part of annual servicing prevents this from developing.

**Temperature differential causing condensation on the exterior.** If the air outside is much warmer and more humid than inside, condensation can form on the surface of the indoor unit or the pipes. This is normal physics, not a fault, but if it's dripping into the room, check that the pipe insulation on the refrigerant lines hasn't deteriorated and is still wrapping the full run from indoor to outdoor unit.

## Problem 4: Daikin Air Conditioner Blinking Light

Blinking lights are often the first sign something isn't right with a Daikin unit, and the operation lamp is a key indicator of system status or faults.

**Green operation light blinking** — This is the primary fault indicator on most Daikin models. A blinking green light typically signals a fault or error has been detected. Count the number of blinks in the sequence, since the blink pattern corresponds directly to specific error codes. Seven blinks then a pause, for example, corresponds to a specific fault. Match the blink count to the error code table in your unit's manual or refer to the error codes section later in this article.

**Orange light on indoor unit** — An orange light is a feature unique to Daikin units and typically indicates maintenance is required, such as cleaning the streamer unit or changing a filter. This is not a fault code — it's a maintenance reminder. Clean the filter and streamer unit (the air purification component found on models with Streamer technology), then reset the maintenance reminder by holding the appropriate button combination listed in your manual.

**Timer light blinking** — A flashing timer light usually means the timer function has been activated or that the unit is waiting to start at a programmed time. Check the timer settings on the remote and cancel them if you want immediate operation.

**All lights flashing simultaneously** — This typically indicates a more serious system fault, often a communication error or high-pressure protection trigger. A full power reset (isolator off for 5 minutes) is the first step. If all lights continue flashing after the reset, the error code needs to be retrieved from the unit.

**How to retrieve a Daikin error code from the remote:** On the ARC433A series remote, hold the Timer Cancel button for 5 seconds until "00" flashes on the temperature display. Press the Timer Cancel button repeatedly until a continuous beep is produced. The code changes in sequence and notifies with a long beep when the fault code is reached. Write down the code before clearing it.

## Problem 5: Daikin Air Conditioner Making Unusual Noise

**Rattling or vibrating:** Almost always a loose panel, a screw that has worked loose over time, or debris inside the outdoor unit. Turn the unit off and check the indoor unit's front panel and filter cover are fully clicked into place. On the outdoor unit, check the grille for any trapped debris like leaves or sticks.

**Buzzing or humming from the outdoor unit:** A low hum during operation is normal from the compressor and fan motor. A loud, irregular buzz or a buzz that changes pitch typically points to a loose fan blade, a failing capacitor, or compressor strain from refrigerant issues.

**Clicking on startup or shutdown:** Normal thermal expansion and contraction of plastic components. Continuous clicking during operation is more concerning and may indicate the compressor is struggling to start.

**Gurgling inside the indoor unit:** The sound of refrigerant moving through the system, particularly after startup. Normal and not a fault.

**Grinding or squealing:** Worn fan motors are a common source of grinding or squealing sounds that typically worsen over time. If the sound is coming from the fan section of either the indoor or outdoor unit, the fan motor bearing is failing and should be inspected by a technician before it fails completely.

**Whistling from the indoor unit:** Almost always airflow restriction — either a dirty filter or furniture blocking the intake grille. Clean the filter first.

## Problem 6: Daikin Air Conditioner Keeps Turning Off

If your Daikin AC keeps turning off unexpectedly, it's often a sign the system is protecting itself from potential damage or there's a minor issue that needs attention.

**High-pressure protection** — The system shuts itself off to prevent damage when the refrigerant pressure inside gets too high. Common triggers include a dirty outdoor condenser coil that can't release heat, a blocked outdoor unit airflow, or refrigerant overcharge (too much refrigerant, which requires a technician to correct).

**Low-pressure protection** — Shuts the unit off when pressure drops too low, most commonly from a refrigerant leak. If the unit repeatedly shuts off after only a short cooling period and the indoor air never gets cold, low refrigerant is the likely cause.

**Dirty filter triggering thermal protection** — A severely clogged filter can reduce airflow enough that the indoor unit overheats and triggers its thermal protection, shutting the whole system down. Clean the filter and run the unit again.

**Sleep timer or economy mode activating** — Daikin's Sleep mode gradually raises the set temperature during the night and eventually switches the unit off. If your unit turns off at a consistent time, check whether Sleep or Economy mode is active on the remote.

**Outdoor unit overheating** — If the outdoor unit is located in direct sun in a very hot climate with no shade or airflow around it, it can overheat during peak afternoon heat. Providing shade without blocking airflow can help, but this is ultimately a positioning problem.

## Problem 7: Daikin Air Conditioner Remote Control Not Working

Replace the batteries first, always. This resolves the majority of remote issues and takes 30 seconds.

If new batteries don't help, check whether the infrared receiver on the indoor unit is dirty or obstructed. Wipe the small receiver window on the front panel of the indoor unit with a dry cloth and confirm there's a clear line of sight between the remote and the unit.

If the unit responds to its physical panel buttons (if it has them) but not the remote, the remote has failed. Daikin replacement remotes are available from authorized dealers and third-party suppliers. Note that Daikin remotes are model-specific on some series — confirm compatibility before purchasing.

On newer Daikin units with Daikin Online Controller or Daikin One+ thermostat compatibility, the smartphone app can control the unit independently of the physical remote, which is worth setting up as a backup on any Wi-Fi-enabled model.

## Daikin Error Codes: Complete Reference Guide

Daikin systems use alphanumeric codes — letters combined with numbers, such as A5, U4, J3 — that display on the indoor unit, remote control, or PCB when the system detects something outside its normal operating range. The letters used include A, C, E, H, F, J, L, P, U, and M. The codes are divided into indoor unit related, outdoor unit related, system related, and sensor related groups.

**First step for any error code:** Power cycle the unit at the isolator or circuit breaker for 5 full minutes. Many error codes caused by temporary faults, power surges, or momentary sensor glitches clear on reset and do not return. If the code clears and doesn't come back, it was a transient fault. If it returns consistently, the component it identifies needs inspection.

### Indoor Unit Error Codes

**A1** — Indoor PCB (control board) failure. The main circuit board in the indoor unit has malfunctioned. Reset first. If it persists, the control board needs replacement by a technician.

**A3** — Indoor coil temperature is outside normal range. Can indicate a refrigerant issue, a coil sensor fault, or airflow restriction. Clean the filter and check for coil freezing first.

**A5** — Freeze protection in cooling mode or high pressure protection in heating mode. Often triggered by low airflow from dirty filters, a blocked air path, low refrigerant, or sensor issues. Clean the filter and check airflow before calling a technician.

**A6 / A7** — Indoor fan not operating correctly. The indoor fan motor has failed or the speed sensor is malfunctioning. Requires a technician.

**A9** — Electronic expansion valve malfunction. The valve that controls refrigerant flow into the indoor unit has failed. Technician required.

**C4** — Indoor coil sensor malfunction. The thermistor monitoring the evaporator coil temperature has failed. Reset first, replace if code returns.

**C5** — Pipe temperature sensor failure. Similar to C4, a thermistor has failed. Reset first, replace if it persists.

**C9** — Return air sensor not working correctly. The sensor reading room temperature has failed. Reset first, replace if the code returns.

### Outdoor Unit Error Codes

**E1** — Control board failure in the outdoor unit. The outdoor PCB has malfunctioned. Technician required.

**E6** — Compressor motor overcurrent or startup failure. The compressor is drawing too much current or failing to start. Commonly caused by a seized compressor, a failed capacitor, or refrigerant issues. Technician required.

**E7** — Outdoor fan not functioning properly. The outdoor condenser fan motor has failed. Technician required, though it's a less expensive repair than compressor work.

**F3** — System pressure is too high. High refrigerant pressure, often caused by a dirty outdoor condenser coil, an obstructed outdoor unit, or refrigerant overcharge. Clean the outdoor coil first. If the code persists, technician required to check refrigerant charge.

**J3** — Outdoor discharge pipe temperature sensor fault. Could be a bad thermistor or wiring issue. Reset first, replace thermistor if the code returns.

### System and Communication Error Codes

**U0** — Insufficient refrigerant in the system. Refrigerant levels are too low, indicating a leak. Technician required to locate the leak, repair it, and recharge.

**U2** — Voltage is outside acceptable range. The power supply to the unit is too high or too low. Check the circuit and contact an electrician if the voltage issue is confirmed.

**U4** — Communication error between the indoor and outdoor units. Signals aren't being shared correctly — could be wiring issues or PCB failure. Check that all wiring connections between the indoor and outdoor unit are secure and undamaged. If wiring is intact, the PCB on either unit may need replacement.

**UA** — Abnormal high pressure trip. Similar to F3 but triggered as a safety protection event rather than a detected measurement. Clean the outdoor unit and check for airflow obstruction.

**UH** — Refrigerant system abnormality. Broad indicator of a refrigerant circuit issue. Technician required for diagnosis.

**L3** — Compressor is under excessive load. The compressor is working harder than it should, often caused by low refrigerant, dirty coils, or a failing expansion valve. Technician required.

**L4** — Compressor not starting or seized. The compressor has failed to start or has mechanically seized. This is the most serious and expensive repair in any AC system. Technician required.

**L5** — Compressor overcurrent during operation. The compressor is drawing excessive current while running, typically a sign of wear or impending failure. Technician required.

**P4** — Heat sink temperature sensor fault on the inverter board. Technician required.

**H6** — Outdoor fan motor malfunction. Same as E7 — the outdoor condenser fan has failed.

## Daikin Recall You Need to Know About

Certain Daikin, Amana, and Goodman branded heat pumps manufactured between specific date ranges were subject to a recall due to a software fault that could cause the unit to switch to heating mode after a power interruption even when the thermostat is set to cooling, posing an excessive heat exposure risk. Affected units can receive a corrective software update remotely for units connected to Wi-Fi. Contact Daikin Comfort Technologies to confirm whether your specific unit is affected and to arrange the update.

## Daikin AC Maintenance Checklist

**Every 2 weeks during heavy use:** Clean the air filter. This single task prevents the majority of cooling problems, coil freezing, noise issues, and bad smells simultaneously.

**Every month during heavy use:** Check the outdoor unit is clear of leaves, grass clippings, and debris. Make sure at least 50cm of clearance exists on all sides of the outdoor unit.

**Once per season:** Clean the streamer unit if your model has one (triggers the orange maintenance light when due). Check that the drain line is flowing freely.

**Once per year:** Professional service including coil cleaning, refrigerant pressure check, electrical connection inspection, and fan motor check. Daikin recommends annual servicing to maintain both performance and warranty validity on most models.

## When to Call a Technician vs Fix It Yourself

**DIY appropriate:**

- Cleaning the air filter and outdoor unit
- Resetting the unit after a power interruption
- Clearing a blocked drain line
- Replacing remote batteries
- Defrosting a frozen coil (switch to fan mode)
- Clearing the orange maintenance light

**Technician required:**

- Any error code that returns after a full power reset
- U0 and UH (refrigerant issues — illegal to handle without certification)
- L4 (seized compressor)
- U4 (communication error that persists after wiring check)
- E6, E7 (outdoor compressor and fan motor)
- Any refrigerant recharge
