Indian cooking produces a lot of oil and smoke, so a kitchen chimney's filters clog quickly. A clogged chimney sucks poorly, makes more noise, drips oil and puts extra strain on the motor. Regular cleaning fixes most of these problems and costs very little. This guide shows how to clean the filters, the oil collector and the body safely at home, how often to do it and when to call a professional.

## How often should you clean a chimney?

| Part | Typical frequency |
|---|---|
| Mesh or cassette filter | Every 1 to 2 weeks with heavy frying, otherwise about monthly |
| Baffle filter | About every 1 to 2 months |
| Oil collector cup | When you notice oil in it, weekly for heavy use |
| Outside body and panel | Weekly wipe |
| Deep clean (service) | Every 3 to 6 months |

Follow your model's manual if it gives a different schedule.

## What you will need

- Hot water, a large tub or basin
- Dishwashing liquid or a degreaser safe for the filter material
- Baking soda (optional)
- Soft brush or old toothbrush
- Microfibre cloth and dry cloth
- Gloves

## Step-by-step: cleaning the filters

1. **Switch off and unplug the chimney.** Let it cool if you have just cooked.
2. **Remove the filters.** Most baffle and cassette filters slide or unclip. Check the manual if yours is stuck.
3. **Soak in hot water with dishwashing liquid** for 15 to 30 minutes. For heavy grease, add a few spoons of baking soda or a degreaser that is safe for the filter material.
4. **Scrub gently** with a soft brush, following the direction of the baffles or mesh. Do not bend the metal.
5. **Rinse well** with warm water until no soap or grease is left.
6. **Dry completely** before refitting, ideally in the sun or in a well-ventilated place. Damp filters smell and collect more grease.

## Cleaning the oil collector

The oil collector cup or tray usually sits at the bottom or side of the chimney. Empty it, wash it with hot soapy water, dry it and refit it. Do not let it overflow, since oil can drip on the stove.

## Cleaning the body and panel

- Wipe the outside with a soft cloth dipped in warm soapy water and wrung out.
- For glass panels, use a glass cleaner or vinegar and water, then a dry cloth.
- Clean buttons and the touch panel with a barely damp cloth. Never spray liquid directly on controls.
- Wipe the inside of the hood where you can reach it without touching the motor.

## Auto-clean chimneys

If your chimney has an auto-clean feature, run the cycle when the manual recommends, often every few weeks. It heats the oil on the inner surface so it drains into the collector. You still need to empty the oil collector and wipe the outside, and you should clean the filters or mesh if your model uses them.

## What not to do

- **Do not wet the motor or electricals.** Never pour water or spray cleaners into the motor housing.
- **Do not use harsh acids or strong bleach** on aluminium mesh, since they can discolour or damage it.
- **Do not use a wire brush** on filters or the body, as it scratches.
- **Do not refit damp filters.**
- **Do not run the chimney with filters removed,** because oil will reach the motor.
- **Do not clean while plugged in.**

## Signs your chimney needs cleaning now

- Weak suction or smoke not clearing
- Oil dripping from the hood
- Louder than usual noise or vibration
- A burnt or greasy smell
- The oil collector is full

## When to call a professional

If the chimney still has weak suction after cleaning, makes grinding noises or the oil keeps leaking, ask the brand's service centre or a technician. Deep servicing also helps with the motor and ducting. A bent or long duct reduces suction, so check that as well.

## Tips to keep the chimney clean longer

- Switch the chimney on a minute before cooking and leave it for a few minutes after.
- Cover pans when frying.
- Wipe splatters on the hob so they do not travel up.
- Keep a schedule on your phone.
