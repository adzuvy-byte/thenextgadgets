The LG OLED evo C5 is LG's mid-premium OLED TV for 2025. It sits below the brighter G-series and above the budget OLEDs, and it is aimed at buyers who want the contrast of OLED with strong gaming support. This review covers the 65-inch model (OLED65C56LA) and draws on LG's specifications and published reviews. We have not tested the TV ourselves.

## Picture quality

Each pixel in an OLED panel lights up on its own, so blacks are truly black and contrast is very high. Reviewers praise the C5 for rich colour, strong HDR and SDR performance, wide viewing angles and no blooming around bright objects. This is the main reason to buy an OLED, and the C5 does it well.

The weak spot is brightness in bright rooms. Like other OLEDs, the C5 can struggle against direct sunlight and reflections. TechRadar measured its peak HDR brightness at about 1,180 nits, though the figure depends on the picture mode and the size of the bright area. The brighter G-series, or the newer C6, is the better pick for very bright rooms.

## Gaming

Gaming is a strength. Published reviews list 4K at 120 Hz, variable refresh rate up to 144 Hz, auto low latency mode, NVIDIA G-Sync and AMD FreeSync Premium, with very low input lag. All four HDMI ports are HDMI 2.1, so you can connect a console, a PC and a soundbar without swapping cables. The Game Optimizer dashboard collects the gaming settings in one place.

## Sound and webOS

The built-in 40 W speakers are clear for dialogue and everyday viewing, but they lack bass and depth. If you watch movies or sports often, plan to add a soundbar. See our [soundbar comparison](/jbl-bar-500-vs-samsung-q800f/) for options.

LG's webOS sets up quickly and supports multiple user profiles with personal recommendations. Reviewers found the Magic Remote cursor less intuitive at first, though most people adjust in a short time.

## C5 versus the newer C6

LG launched the 2026 C6 as the successor. Both come in the same sizes and share the same design and four HDMI 2.1 ports. Published reviews describe the C6 as a clear but not dramatic upgrade, with the biggest gains in the processor, brightness and refresh rate.

Neither model supports HDR10+, and the consoles still output at 120 Hz, so the higher 165 Hz ceiling mainly helps PC gamers. The brightness figures come from one reviewer's measurements and will differ between tests. Note that the larger 77 and 83 inch C6 models use a different, brighter panel type than the 55 and 65 inch models.

**Which should you buy?** Reviewers say the C6 is the better TV, but the C5 is the better value when it is on sale. If the C6 costs only slightly more in your size, take the C6. If the C5 has a large saving, the C5 is still a very capable OLED. Check both listings for the current price.

## Verdict

The LG OLED evo C5 is a balanced premium OLED. It gives you the contrast and black levels OLED is known for, strong gaming support and four HDMI 2.1 ports. It is only a modest step up from the C4, the speakers are weak, and bright rooms are a weak spot. If you can control the light in your room and pair it with a soundbar, it is a strong choice. The C6 is the better TV on paper, so compare the two prices before you buy.
