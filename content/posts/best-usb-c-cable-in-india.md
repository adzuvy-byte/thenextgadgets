## How to choose a USB-C cable

- **Wattage.** A phone needs 20 to 45 W. Most laptops need 45 to 100 W. A cable rated for 60 W will cap a 100 W laptop charger at 60 W.
- **Data speed.** Many charging cables carry data at USB 2.0 speed (480 Mbps). If you move files or connect drives or displays, look for a cable that lists 5, 10 or 20 Gbps or Thunderbolt.
- **E-marker chip.** Cables above 60 W need a chip that tells the charger the cable can carry more current. A 100 W-rated cable will have one.
- **Length.** Longer cables are convenient but can drop some power. 1 m is usually enough for a desk.
- **Build.** Braiding and reinforced ends help with bending, but check the real warranty.
- **Charger and device.** Cable, charger and device each cap the speed. The slowest of the three wins.
