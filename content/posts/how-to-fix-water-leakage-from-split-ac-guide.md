Some water from a split AC is normal. It is condensation, and it should flow out through the drain pipe. If water drips from the indoor unit, runs down the wall or collects on the floor, something is blocking or disturbing that path. The good news is that the cause is often simple and fixable. This guide covers the most common causes, what you can check safely yourself and when to call a technician.

## Why does a split AC leak water?

| Cause | What you will notice |
|---|---|
| Clogged drain pipe | Water dripping steadily from the indoor unit or the pipe joint |
| Dirty air filter | Weak cooling, ice on the coil, then water when it melts |
| Wrong installation slope | The indoor unit is tilted so water overflows instead of draining |
| Low refrigerant | Ice on the pipes or coil, then dripping when it thaws |
| Damaged or full drain pan | Water dripping from underneath the unit |
| Loose or kinked drain hose | Water at the joint or a kink in the pipe |
| High humidity | Heavy condensation, especially in monsoon |
| Poor pipe insulation | Water beading and dripping along the pipes |

## Safety first

Switch the AC off and use the remote or the MCB to cut the power before you touch anything. Do not poke around electrical parts, and do not stand on an unstable chair. Never try to refill gas yourself. That needs a trained technician.

## Step 1: Clean the air filter

A dirty filter limits airflow, so the coil gets too cold and freezes. When the ice melts, it overflows the tray.

1. Open the front panel and slide the filters out.
2. Rinse them under water or vacuum the dust, using mild soap if greasy.
3. Dry them completely and refit them.

Clean the filters every 2 to 4 weeks during heavy use.

## Step 2: Check for ice

If the coil or pipe has ice on it, turn the AC to fan mode for a few hours to melt it. Then clean the filters and run it again. If ice returns, the cause could be low refrigerant or a faulty part, and you will need a technician.

## Step 3: Check the drain pipe

Follow the drain pipe from the indoor unit to the outside.

- Look for kinks, bends or places where it sags.
- Check that the outlet is not submerged or blocked by dirt, insects or algae.
- The pipe should slope downwards all the way outside.

If the pipe is clogged, a technician can clear it with a vacuum or a pressure flush. Do not push a stiff wire inside, since it can pierce the hose.

## Step 4: Check the indoor unit slope

The indoor unit should be level or tilted very slightly towards the drain side, as the installer sets it. If it is tilted the wrong way, water will drip from the front instead of draining. A technician can reposition it.

## Step 5: Look at pipe insulation

If water drips along the copper pipes inside the room, the foam insulation may be missing or damaged. Re-wrapping the pipes with insulation tape stops condensation dripping.

## Step 6: Reduce humidity load

During humid weather, keep doors and windows closed while the AC runs, and avoid setting a very low temperature. Heavy humidity can cause more condensation.

## When to call a technician

- Ice keeps coming back after cleaning the filter.
- You suspect low refrigerant or a gas leak.
- The drain pipe is blocked or the unit needs to be re-levelled.
- Water drips near electrical parts.
- The AC is under warranty. Check the terms, because a self-repair could affect it.

## How to prevent leakage

- Clean the filters regularly.
- Get a yearly service before summer, which should include a drain flush.
- Make sure the drain pipe slopes properly and is secure.
- Insulate exposed pipes.
- Do not block the airflow in front of the indoor unit.
