Water purifiers fail slowly, which makes them easy to ignore until the water tastes wrong or the flow drops. Most problems come from filters that are overdue, low inlet pressure, or a component that has aged. This guide separates what you can check from what you should leave to a technician.

**A caution on safety.** A purifier is there to make drinking water safe. If you suspect it is no longer doing that, such as a failed UV lamp, a stale taste or a smell that does not clear after a service, do not depend on it. Boil the water or use another safe source until it is checked.

## Problem 1: Low or slow flow

**Likely causes**
- Clogged sediment or carbon pre-filter
- RO membrane near the end of its life
- Low inlet water pressure or a partly closed inlet valve
- A pinched or kinked pipe

**What to check**
- Is the inlet tap fully open, and is there enough pressure at the wall?
- Is the filter replacement overdue? Check the maker's schedule or the service reminder.
- Is the tube kinked behind the unit?

If the filters are old, replacement is the fix. Have it done by service, and ask for the membrane to be checked rather than replaced by default.

## Problem 2: Bad taste or smell

**Likely causes**
- Overdue carbon or other filters
- Stale water sitting in the tank, especially after a holiday
- Poorly cleaned tank

**What to check**
- Drain the tank and let it refill if it has been unused for several days.
- Check when filters were last replaced.
- Ask service to sanitise the tank if the smell persists.

## Problem 3: No water

**Likely causes**
- No inlet water or low pressure
- Power not reaching the unit or the adapter
- Tank full (the unit stops on purpose)
- Pump or solenoid fault

**What to check**
- Is there incoming water? Is the unit powered and is the indicator on?
- Is the tank actually full? Run a little water out and see if it restarts.

A humming noise with no water points to the pump or a low inlet pressure issue.

## Problem 4: Constant dripping or draining

RO purifiers always send some water to drain as reject. If the drain never stops, even with a full tank, the auto shut-off valve may be stuck. Close the inlet, and call service.

## Problem 5: Leaks

Check for loose push-fit connectors at the back and under the unit, and a cracked housing. Close the inlet valve and unplug the unit if there is a steady leak. A leak near the electrical parts needs service before you use the unit again.

## Problem 6: Noisy pump

Some hum is normal. Loud rattling or squealing usually means low inlet pressure, a failing pump or air in the line. Do not keep running a noisy pump dry.

## Problem 7: UV light not working

In a UV purifier the lamp is part of disinfection. If the indicator is off or flashing, get it checked. Until then, boil the water if it is your only source.

## Simple habits that help

- Note the date of each filter change.
- Use water within a day or two, and flush the tank after a long absence.
- Keep the unit away from heat and direct sun.
- Choose a purifier that matches your water source. Check your water's TDS and, if you can, get it tested, because the right technology depends on it.
