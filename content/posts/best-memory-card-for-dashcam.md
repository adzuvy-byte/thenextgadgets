## FAQs: Best Memory Card for Dashcam

### 1. What is the Best memory card for dashcam?

The Best memory card for dashcam is a high-endurance microSD card like Samsung PRO Endurance or SanDisk High Endurance. These cards are designed for continuous recording and last longer than standard memory cards.

### 2. Can I use a normal SD card in a dashcam?

Yes, you can use a normal SD card, but it is not recommended. Dashcams continuously overwrite data, which can quickly wear out regular cards. High-endurance cards are a better choice for long-term reliability.

### 3. How much storage is Best for a dashcam?

A 128GB or 256GB memory card is ideal for most dashcams. It provides enough storage for several hours of footage before overwriting old recordings.

### 4. Is Samsung EVO Plus good for dashcam?

Yes, the Samsung EVO Plus is a good budget option for dashcams. It offers reliable performance for 1080p and moderate use, but it is not as durable as high-endurance cards.

### 5. What speed card is required for a dashcam?

A U3 or V30 speed class memory card is recommended for dashcams, especially for 4K recording. This ensures smooth video capture without lag or frame drops.

### 6. How long does a dashcam memory card last?

A high-endurance memory card can last 2–5 years depending on usage, while a regular SD card may fail within a few months under continuous recording.

### 7. Which is better: SanDisk or Samsung for dashcam?

Both brands are reliable. Samsung offers excellent overall performance, while SanDisk provides strong endurance-focused options. The choice depends on your budget and usage.

### 8. Do dashcams need special SD cards?

Yes, dashcams work Best with high-endurance microSD cards because they are designed for constant recording and extreme conditions.

### 9. How often should I replace my dashcam SD card?

It’s recommended to replace your dashcam memory card every 1–2 years to avoid data loss, especially if you use it daily.

### 10. What happens if I use a low-quality memory card in a dashcam?

Low-quality memory cards may cause recording errors, corrupted footage, or sudden failure, which can result in lost video evidence.
