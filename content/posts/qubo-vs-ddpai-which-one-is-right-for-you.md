## Who makes them

**Qubo** is the connected-device brand of Hero Electronix, an Indian company. Its dash cams are sold on Qubo's own website and on Amazon, and they are managed through the Qubo Pro app. The range now runs from a very cheap 2K model up to 4K, three-channel and 4G models.

**DDPAI** is a Chinese company that focuses on dash cams. In India its cameras are sold through Amazon.in, Flipkart and its own India store. Its range here includes the True 4K Dual Channel, and the newer Z50 Dual 4K and Z40 Dual 3K that launched in July 2024.

## What each brand sells in India

### Qubo

- **Dashcam Pro 2K:** 3MP QHD (1296p) video, 120° field of view, built-in Wi-Fi, no GPS listed, no SD card included. Listed at ₹3,990 against an MRP of ₹6,990.
- **Dashcam Pro 2.7K:** 2.7K QHD+ with a dual-camera setup and a full HD rear camera. Reported at ₹7,990 at launch in May 2025.
- **Dashcam Pro 3K:** Sony STARVIS 2 sensor, a 2MP rear camera and a 3.2-inch screen that switches between front and rear views. Reported at ₹10,990.
- **Dashcam Pro 4K:** 4K UHD from an 8MP Sony IMX415 sensor, F1.8, 140° field of view, built-in GPS, a 2.8-inch screen and ADAS warnings. Qubo's page lists it at ₹14,990 against an MRP of ₹22,990, and the rear camera is sold in a bundle.
- **Dashcam 4G Live (₹15,990) and Dashcam Trio (₹14,990):** announced in January 2026. The 4G Live streams over 4G and sends collision, overspeed and geofence alerts. The Trio records the road ahead in 2K plus the road behind and the cabin in 1080p.

### DDPAI

- **True 4K Dual Channel (2026):** a dual-channel (front and rear) 4K dash cam listed on Amazon.in at around ₹11,999 when we checked (2026-11-10), rated 4.2/5 by 642 buyers. Check the listing for GPS, card limit and parking mode.
- **Z50 Dual 4K and Z40 Dual 3K:** launched in July 2024 at MRPs of ₹19,990 and ₹15,990, with launch offers of ₹14,990 and ₹9,990 on Amazon.in. Both list 8MP IMX415 sensors, a 140° lens at f/1.75, a 2.3-inch screen, GPS, a 3-axis gyro for parking incidents and cards up to 512GB.

The Pro X is the simpler, cheaper camera. It records at 2K, can face into the cabin (useful for cabs and ride-share), has a supercapacitor and takes cards up to 1TB. It has no GPS and no separate rear camera. The DDPAI True 4K Dual Channel gives you front and rear recording in one package at a higher price, so check its card limit and GPS support on the listing.

## Where DDPAI's Z50 and Z40 fit

If you want DDPAI at 4K, the Z50 is also worth a look. Its listed specs are very close to the Qubo Pro 4K: the same 8MP IMX415 class sensor, a 140° lens and built-in GPS. It adds a rear camera as standard and a 3-axis gyro for recording during parking incidents.

That is also why we will not claim one brand has better image quality. When two cameras share a sensor, differences come from the lens, image processing, windshield position and how well the camera is installed. Only side-by-side footage can settle that, and we have not shot it.

## What to check before you buy any dash cam

1. **Rear camera.** Check whether the listing includes it. Qubo's 4K rear camera is a separate bundle and the cable must be routed along the roof lining to the back glass.
2. **Parking mode.** Dash cams in general need a hardwire kit for proper parking recording; check each listing. A hardwire kit is fitted to the fuse box, which most people have done by an installer.
3. **Memory card.** Check whether one is included. Qubo's Pro 2K page says it is not. Buy a high-endurance microSD card designed for continuous recording, and format it in the camera from time to time.
4. **Supercapacitor, not battery.** Both brands use supercapacitors, which handle heat better than lithium batteries. That matters on a dashboard in an Indian summer.
5. **ADAS.** Lane and collision warnings depend on correct mounting and calibration. Do not rely on them.
6. **Subscriptions.** The Qubo 4G Live uses a CloudPlay subscription for cloud features. Check the price before you buy.
7. **Warranty.** Neither Qubo's 4K page nor DDPAI's page stated a warranty period when we checked. Read the listing and ask the seller.

## Our verdict

For most Indian drivers who want a solid 4K camera with GPS and ADAS, the Qubo Dashcam Pro 4K has the stronger spec sheet at the price it is listed for. If you want a low-cost 2K camera, the Qubo Dashcam Pro X (2026 Edition) is the simpler choice, with a cabin-facing option and 1TB card support. If you want a rear camera included as standard, the DDPAI Z50 is the closer match to the Qubo Pro 4K, while the True 4K Dual Channel is the current dual-channel DDPAI listed on Amazon.in.

If budget is the main concern, Qubo's Dashcam Pro 2K at ₹3,990 is the lowest-priced current model we found from either brand, though we did not check DDPAI's entry-level Mini. It has no GPS and no parking mode listed, so think of it as a simple loop recorder.

If you want remote monitoring, only Qubo's 4G Live in this comparison offers it, and that comes with a subscription.
