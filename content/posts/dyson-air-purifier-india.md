The machines look like bladeless fans, cost from about ₹27,000 to ₹69,000, and are sold through dyson.in, Dyson stores and, for some models, Amazon.in. This guide sets out the current Indian lineup, what the numbers do and do not tell you, how Dyson compares with regular purifiers costing under half as much, and who should actually buy one.

## What Dyson promises

The Dyson Purifier Cool line uses a sealed H13 HEPA filter plus an activated carbon layer. Dyson says the filter captures 99.95% of particles as small as 0.1 microns. The machine senses PM2.5, PM10 and gases, shows live readings on a screen and in the MyDyson app, and works with Alexa, Google Assistant and Siri. Air is thrown out through a bladeless loop, oscillating up to 350 degrees, so the unit works as a purifier and a fan. The Hot+Cool models also heat, which is useful in North Indian winters.

## The numbers that matter, and one that does not exist

Dyson quotes **airflow of over 290 litres per second**. That is not a CADR. Airflow measures how much air leaves the machine, while CADR measures how much clean air the machine delivers, after the filter, in standard tests. Dyson does not publish a CADR in India on the pages we checked, so you cannot compare it with a Coway or a Qubo on the same scale.

You can convert for a rough idea. 290 litres per second is about 1,044 cubic metres per hour of air moved, but the figure is for the fan, not for clean air. A purifier with a published CADR of 450 m³/h, such as the Qubo Q600, is telling you something more directly useful.

Dyson also says the TP10 was tested in an 81 cubic metre room, about 290 sq ft at a 10 ft ceiling. That is a mid-size bedroom or small living room, and the practical limit for one machine.

## Filter life and running cost

Dyson says the filter lasts about 12 months at 12 hours of use per day. For a purifier used all day in smog, plan to replace it sooner. We could not find a price for the replacement filter on the pages we reviewed, so ask Dyson India before you buy. Lack of a published filter price is a real cost risk on a premium machine.

## Dyson vs ordinary air purifiers

Here is the honest comparison. The Dyson Purifier Cool costs about ₹34,000 to ₹40,000, and the Hushjet HJ10 ₹29,900 on Amazon.in. For that money:

- A **Qubo Q600** costs ₹14,990 with a CADR of 450 m³/h and claimed coverage of 600 sq ft.
- A **Coway Airmega 150** costs ₹14,999, rated 4.7 out of 5 by 6,821 buyers, with a ₹3,299 filter.
- A **Philips AC3220/60** costs ₹22,480, with a CADR of 520 m³/h and coverage of 600 sq ft.

On rated clean air delivered per rupee, the regular purifiers win clearly. Dyson's advantages are different: a fan and purifier in one footprint, a screen and gas sensing, design, app control, and the Dyson retail and service network. If you value those, the premium may make sense. If your priority is clearing smog from a bedroom, you can spend less and move more clean air.

## Who should buy a Dyson?

**Consider a Dyson if:**
- You want one machine that purifies and cools (or heats) a room, and you would otherwise buy a tower fan.
- Appearance, a quiet sleek design and an air quality display matter to you.
- You prefer buying from a brand store with its own support.

**Skip it if:**
- The main goal is the best filtration per rupee, especially in Delhi NCR winters.
- You want a published CADR so you can size the machine to the room.
- You need the filter price confirmed before purchase.

## Our verdict

Dyson air purifiers are premium all-in-one machines. They are well made, and they are priced for design, features and brand rather than for filtering power. If clean air in a smoggy city is the goal, spend the difference on a high-CADR purifier and, if you like, a separate fan. If you want a single sleek device and the cost is acceptable, the Purifier Cool line is the one to look at. Check the filter price and warranty before you pay.
