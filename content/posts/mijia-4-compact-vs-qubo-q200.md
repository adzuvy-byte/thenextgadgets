Mijia 4 Compact vs Qubo Q200 is a matchup between the two cheapest app-connected air purifiers from names that Indian buyers recognise: Xiaomi's Mijia and Hero's Qubo. Both are small, both target bedrooms and study rooms, both cost under ₹9,000, and both claim a True HEPA-class filter. The Mijia is cheaper and moves more air. The Qubo has a longer filter story and a fuller filter stack on paper. We went through both Amazon.in listings on 13 Nov 2026 to see which one deserves your ₹7,000 to ₹9,000.

We have not tested either purifier. What follows is built from the listings, the makers' stated specs and straightforward arithmetic. Where a number is missing we say so instead of guessing.

## The short answer

- **For most bedrooms, buy the Mijia 4 Compact.** It is ₹1,760 cheaper and its airflow is 53% higher (230 m³/h against 150 m³/h).
- **Buy the Qubo Q200** only if you have a very small room (a study or a kids' room under about 110 sq ft) and the five-layer filter, AI mode or a live PM2.5 number in the app matter to you.
- **If the room is larger than about 200 sq ft,** neither is the right size. Look at the [best air purifier under ₹10,000](/best-air-purifier-under-10000/) for bigger options.

## Airflow: the difference that matters most

CADR, short for clean air delivery rate, tells you how much smoke-sized particle-free air a purifier produces each hour. It is the single best number for comparing purifiers, and these two are not close. The Mijia 4 Compact rates 230 m³/h. The Q200's listing says 150 m³/h.

To turn that into a room size, divide CADR by 5 to get the room volume (in cubic metres) it can clean five times an hour, the rate most people want in a polluted city. With a 10 ft ceiling:

- Mijia 4 Compact: 230 ÷ 5 = 46 m³, about **160 sq ft**.
- Qubo Q200: 150 ÷ 5 = 30 m³, about **105 sq ft**.

Xiaomi's lower coverage figure (172 sq ft) sits close to that five-changes-an-hour result, which suggests the maker used a sensible method for the low end of its range. Qubo's 200 sq ft claim works out to about 2.6 air changes an hour at a 10 ft ceiling. That is acceptable for ordinary air but thin in heavy winter smog.

So the two are really aimed at different rooms. The Mijia suits an ordinary 12 ft by 13 ft bedroom. The Q200 suits a box room, a study, a nursery or a bathroom-sized space. Put a Q200 in a 180 sq ft bedroom in Delhi in December and it will run flat out for a long time without ever catching up.

## Filters: fuller stack on paper, but one unknown on each

Qubo's listing describes five layers: two pre-filters, a nano-silver antibacterial coating, activated carbon and a True HEPA H13 filter, with a stated life of up to 9,000 hours. Xiaomi describes a three-in-one filter that it says captures 99.97% of particles down to 0.3 microns, backed by TÜV Rheinland Allergy Care certification. More layers on a listing does not automatically mean cleaner air, because the HEPA stage and the airflow do most of the work. The practical differences are:

- **The Qubo lists activated carbon.** That helps with smoke and cooking smells to a degree. Xiaomi's description mentions odours too but does not name a carbon layer, so check the product page if smell removal is your priority.
- **The 9,000-hour figure is a ceiling.** At 12 hours a day it works out to about two years. In smoggy cities you will clog the pre-filters and the HEPA layer sooner, so plan on yearly replacement and clean the pre-filters every few weeks.
- **Replacement prices are unclear for both.** We could not find a genuine filter for the Mijia 4 Compact on Amazon.in on 13 Nov 2026. For the Qubo, genuine filters are listed for the Q400 (₹2,290) and the Q600 (₹3,390) but not for the Q200. Message the seller and ask for the genuine filter price and its availability before you pay.

That last point is the biggest unknown in this comparison. A purifier is a razor-and-blades product, and a ₹7,000 machine with a ₹3,500 filter is not cheap.

## Noise and sleep

Xiaomi states 20 dB in sleep mode with the display lights off, which is the quietest class of purifier and fine for a nursery. Qubo does not give a decibel figure for the Q200 on the Amazon.in page. A lower-airflow fan can be quieter, but we cannot tell you that from a listing. If light sleepers share the room, buy from a seller with an easy return policy and test it at night.

## App, sensors and daily use

Both purifiers connect to a phone app and respond to Alexa and Google Assistant. The difference is in how they report air quality:

- The **Mijia** shows PM2.5 as a four-colour light on the unit and runs a one-touch auto mode that adjusts the fan. The Xiaomi Home app adds timers, child lock and remote control.
- The **Qubo** gives a live PM2.5 number in the app and offers four modes, including an AI mode that switches the purifier on and off as air quality changes. If you like numbers and automation, that is a real plus.

Both need a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi network for the app, so check your router before setup. Both can also be used from the buttons without Wi-Fi.

## What the ratings tell you (and do not)

Look closely and you will see something odd: the Mijia 4 Compact and the Mijia Purifier 6 both show 4.2 stars from 1,014 ratings, and the Qubo Q200 and Q600 both show 4.0 from 1,421. Amazon.in groups variants, so those numbers are a brand-level impression rather than a verdict on each model. Both are decent, neither is outstanding, and we would not decide between these two on a 0.2-star gap.

## Who should buy which

**Buy the Mijia 4 Compact if:**
- The room is a bedroom or home office of about 100 to 170 sq ft.
- You want the lowest price from a major brand with an app.
- A published 20 dB sleep figure matters, for a nursery for instance.

**Buy the Qubo Q200 if:**
- The room is very small, below about 110 sq ft.
- You want activated carbon on the spec sheet, a 9,000-hour filter rating and an AI mode.
- You prefer to see a PM2.5 number in the app.

**Buy neither if:** you need to clean a living room or a large bedroom.
