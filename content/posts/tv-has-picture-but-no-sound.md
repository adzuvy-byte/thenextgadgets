Your TV has picture but no sound — the screen is working perfectly, you can see everything clearly, but complete silence is coming from the speakers. Before you assume something is seriously broken, take a breath. This is one of the most common TV complaints across every brand and every age of television, and the overwhelming majority of cases come down to a setting, a cable, or a software state that took itself somewhere unexpected. Actual hardware failure is the least likely explanation, not the first one.

This guide walks through every real cause in the correct order — starting with the simplest checks that take ten seconds and moving toward the more involved ones — so you find the actual problem rather than spending an hour trying things at random.

## The Diagnostic Question That Saves You the Most Time

Before touching a single button, answer this: does the silence affect everything on your TV, or just one specific input or app?

If you switch inputs — from your cable box to a streaming stick, from HDMI 1 to HDMI 2, from Netflix to the built-in tuner — and sound works on some sources but not others, the TV's speakers and audio hardware are completely fine. The problem is isolated to that one source, cable, port, or app, not the television itself. This single diagnostic cuts the troubleshooting in half and tells you exactly where to look.

If there is no sound from anything — every input, every app, the built-in tuner, even the TV's menu sounds — the problem is either a setting that has been changed, a software glitch, or in rarer cases, a hardware fault. Work through the sections below in order.

## Fix 1: The Settings Checks Everyone Skips Because They Feel Too Obvious

These account for a genuinely surprising percentage of "TV has no sound" situations, including for people who consider themselves tech-savvy. Do not skip them.

**Check the mute button.** Press mute on your remote once and see if the mute icon appears or disappears on screen. Some TV remotes have a mute button that is easy to press accidentally, and some TVs display no on-screen mute indicator at all, making it genuinely invisible unless you know to look for it. Turn the volume up using the volume buttons to confirm.

**Check that the volume is not at zero.** This is different from mute. Some TV interfaces allow the volume bar to be at zero without showing a mute icon. Press the volume up button five or six times and watch the on-screen volume bar to confirm it is actually moving.

**Check the audio output setting — this is the most commonly overlooked cause.** On a Samsung, Sound Output typically offers TV Speaker, Soundbar (HDMI) and a list of Bluetooth devices. If you are not sure where the audio is currently being routed, set it back to TV Speaker — if sound returns, the TV itself is fine and the problem lies somewhere in your external audio chain.

This setting gets changed more often than people realise — a firmware update, a new device connection, or even a family member pairing Bluetooth earphones can silently switch the audio output away from the TV speakers. Find this setting:

- **Samsung:** Settings → Sound → Sound Output → TV Speaker
- **LG:** Settings → Sound → Sound Out → Internal TV Speaker
- **Sony Bravia:** Settings → Display & Sound → Audio Output → TV Speakers
- **TCL/Hisense:** Settings → Sound → Audio Output → TV Speaker

If your TV was routing audio to a soundbar, a Bluetooth speaker, or a set of Bluetooth headphones that are now off or out of range, switching back to TV Speaker restores sound immediately.

**Check SAP (Secondary Audio Program) mode.** SAP mode is a feature that allows TVs to broadcast in alternate audio tracks, typically a secondary language. When accidentally activated, it can result in no audio if the secondary track is empty or silent. This is one of the most under-discussed causes of sudden TV silence, and it catches people completely off guard because they have no idea they activated it.

SAP mode is usually toggled by an Audio or MTS button on the remote, or found inside Settings → Sound → Audio Language or Broadcast Audio. If yours shows SAP, Secondary, or a language other than your primary language selected, switch it back to Main or your default language.

## Fix 2: Power Cycle the TV Properly

There is a specific way to power cycle a TV that actually clears the audio processing state, and it is different from just pressing the power button.

Unplug the TV's power cable from the wall socket completely. Do not use standby — physically unplug it. Then press and hold the physical power button on the TV itself (not the remote) for 30 seconds. This drains any residual charge from the capacitors and clears the audio processor's state more thoroughly than a standby restart. Leave it unplugged for a full 60 seconds, then plug back in and power on normally.

Hold the power button down for 30 seconds after it's unplugged. This step is what most people miss when they say "I already tried turning it off and on" — they used standby, which does not clear the audio processing state.

This single step resolves a meaningful percentage of sudden audio failures caused by software freezes, firmware update glitches, and corrupted audio state — all without touching any settings.

## Fix 3: Check Every Cable — Specifically HDMI

Loose or failing cables are a frequent cause of audio loss. Reseat all HDMI and audio cables, then test with a known good cable if possible. Optical cables should be checked for cracks, tight bends, or debris at the connector. HDMI cables can fail in subtle ways, sometimes passing video without audio or the opposite, so replacing the cable is often a quick and inexpensive fix.

HDMI carries both video and audio in a single cable, and the two signals travel on different pin groups within the same connector. When an HDMI cable develops a partial fault — from being bent at a sharp angle, from the connector being slightly loose, or simply from age — it is entirely possible for the video signal to remain intact while the audio pins fail. This explains exactly the symptom you are experiencing: perfect picture, zero sound.

**What to check:**

Unplug the HDMI cable from both the TV and the source device (streaming stick, cable box, game console) and firmly reseat both ends. Even cables that look seated sometimes are not making full pin contact.

Try the cable in a different HDMI port on the TV. Most TVs have two to four HDMI ports and they behave slightly differently — port 1 is often the ARC/eARC port and carries audio back to a soundbar, while the others carry audio from the source device to the TV. If you accidentally plugged your cable box into the ARC port, audio routing can behave unexpectedly.

If you have a spare HDMI cable, swap it entirely. Cables cost very little and are genuinely the second most common cause of this exact symptom after settings errors.

**For optical audio cables:** The TOSLINK connectors on optical cables are easy to damage and accumulate dust. Remove the cable from both ends, check for bent pins or debris in the connector, and firmly reseat. A cracked optical cable will fail silently — no visible breakage on the outside, no signal on the inside.

## Fix 4: Isolate the Source Device

Audio problems can appear isolated when a single app, HDMI device, or input fails. Switch between streaming apps, HDMI ports, and built-in tuners if available. If sound works on one source but not another, the TV hardware is likely fine and the problem points to the external device, app, or cable.

Work through this systematically:

**Switch to a different input.** If you normally watch through a cable box on HDMI 1, switch to HDMI 2 and connect a different device — a game console, a laptop, or another streaming stick. If audio works on the new input, the problem is with the original cable box, the cable connecting it, or the HDMI port it was using.

**Try a built-in app.** Open Netflix, YouTube, or any built-in streaming app directly from the TV's smart platform without going through an external device. If audio works here, the TV's internal speakers and audio system are confirmed working, and the fault is in an external device or connection.

**Test the TV's built-in tuner.** If your TV is connected to an aerial or cable input, switch to the live TV tuner and check whether broadcast channels have sound. If they do, your speakers are fine.

**Connect a different device to the same HDMI port.** This confirms whether the problem is the port or the original device.

## Fix 5: Check Your Soundbar or External Speaker Connection

If you use a soundbar or external speakers, the TV's internal speakers may have been deliberately disabled when the external audio was set up — which means when the soundbar has a problem, the TV appears to have no sound at all.

**For soundbars connected via HDMI ARC or eARC:** Switch off, unplug, wait, plug back in. Do the soundbar, set-top box and console at the same time — a failed ARC handshake often only recovers when both ends restart together. HDMI ARC establishes a two-way communication handshake between the TV and soundbar. When this handshake fails — from a power interruption, a firmware update on either device, or simply a glitch — the TV routes audio to the soundbar but the soundbar never receives it, resulting in complete silence from both the soundbar and the TV speakers since internal speakers were disabled.

After the full power cycle of both devices, go to your TV's audio output settings and confirm the soundbar is being detected. If it shows as connected but still produces no sound, try switching temporarily to TV Speaker to confirm the TV itself works, then switch back to the soundbar.

**For soundbars connected via optical cable:** Check the optical cable connections as described above. Also confirm the soundbar's input is set to match the connection you are using — a soundbar with optical, HDMI ARC, and Bluetooth inputs will produce no sound if it is set to Bluetooth while the optical cable is connected.

**For Bluetooth speakers:** If a Bluetooth speaker was paired with the TV and is now off, out of range, or out of battery, the TV may still be trying to route audio to it and producing nothing from its own speakers. Go to Bluetooth settings on the TV and either connect to the TV speakers explicitly or disconnect the Bluetooth device.

## Fix 6: Run the TV's Built-In Speaker Test

Most modern smart TVs include a built-in speaker diagnostic in the settings menu. This plays a test tone directly from the TV's audio hardware, bypassing every input, cable, and connected device. If the test tone plays correctly, the speakers are working and the problem is definitively in your signal chain somewhere. If the test tone is also silent, the speakers or the internal audio board have an issue.

- **Samsung:** Settings → Support → Self Diagnosis → Sound Test
- **LG:** Settings → Support → Self Diagnosis → Sound Test
- **Sony:** Settings → Display & Sound → Advanced Settings → Manual (depending on model year)
- **TCL:** Settings → System → Diagnostics → Speaker Test

If you hear the test sound, your speakers work fine. The problem is with your input source or settings. If there's no test sound, continue troubleshooting.

## Fix 7: Update the Firmware

Audio-format compatibility problems, eARC and Atmos especially, genuinely do get fixed in firmware. If you recently connected a new device, changed your HDMI setup, or started experiencing silence after the TV updated itself, a firmware issue is a real possibility in both directions — either a buggy update introduced the problem, or a pending update has the fix.

Find firmware updates in your TV settings:

- **Samsung:** Settings → Support → Software Update → Update Now
- **LG:** Settings → Support → Software Update → Check for Updates
- **Sony:** Settings → Device Preferences → About → System Update
- **TCL/Hisense:** Settings → About TV → Check for System Update

If a firmware update introduced the silence: check the manufacturer's support forum for your specific model number and the current firmware version. If other owners report the same issue after the same update, a corrective firmware is usually pushed within a few weeks. In the meantime, some manufacturers allow rolling back to a previous firmware version through a USB update — check the support page for your model.

## Fix 8: Check App-Specific Audio Settings

If the silence only affects one specific app — Netflix is silent but YouTube has sound, or Hulu is fine but Disney+ is silent — the problem is not the TV at all. It is the app's internal audio settings or a conflict between the app's audio format and your TV's decoding capability.

**Dolby Atmos and Dolby Digital Plus compatibility:** Some streaming apps default to outputting Dolby Atmos or Dolby Digital Plus audio, which requires the TV to support the specific codec. If your TV does not support the format the app is trying to output, some TVs simply produce silence rather than falling back to standard stereo.

Go into the app's settings (usually under Audio or Playback Settings) and change the audio format to Stereo or Auto instead of Dolby or Surround. If sound immediately returns, the issue is codec incompatibility between the app and your TV, and you can either keep it on Stereo, update your TV firmware (which often adds codec support), or connect a soundbar that handles the decoding externally.

**For Netflix specifically:** Netflix has its own audio settings inside the app. Go to the Netflix app → Profile → App Settings → Playback Specifications and check the audio format setting.

## Fix 9: Factory Reset as a Last Software Resort

If every setting check, cable swap, power cycle, and firmware update has been tried and the TV still has picture but no sound from its built-in speakers, a factory reset clears every accumulated setting, corrupted preference, and software state that may be causing the problem.

**Important:** A factory reset removes all your apps, logins, Wi-Fi passwords, picture calibrations, and preferences. Write down your important settings before proceeding.

- **Samsung:** Settings → General → Reset → Enter PIN (default 0000) → Reset
- **LG:** Settings → General → Reset to Initial Settings
- **Sony:** Settings → Device Preferences → Reset → Factory Data Reset
- **TCL/Hisense:** Settings → System → Advanced System Settings → Factory Reset

After the reset, go through the initial setup and test audio before reinstalling apps and logging into accounts. If sound returns after the reset, the problem was a corrupted setting or software state — not hardware.

## When It Is Actually a Hardware Problem

If the built-in speaker test from Fix 6 produced no sound, and a factory reset from Fix 9 didn't restore it, and the TV is definitely not routing audio to an external device — you are most likely dealing with one of two hardware failures:

**Blown or disconnected internal speakers.** The physical speaker drivers inside the TV have failed or become disconnected from the audio board. This occasionally happens after a power surge, from physical vibration over time, or simply from age. A technician can confirm this within minutes and the speakers themselves are often an inexpensive replacement.

**Failed audio processor or T-Con board.** A professional inspection is recommended to determine if the issue lies with the backlight strips, T-Con board, or the motherboard. The audio processing chip on the main board can fail from heat, a power surge, or simply from age, producing exactly this symptom — perfect picture, complete silence. This is a more involved and more expensive repair.

**Practical advice on hardware repairs:** For a TV under warranty, contact the manufacturer before any repair. For an out-of-warranty TV, get a repair estimate first — if the repair cost exceeds half the price of a comparable new TV, replacement is usually the more economical choice. A local TV repair technician can typically diagnose the specific fault within 30 minutes.

## The Complete Fix Order — Quick Reference

If you want the fastest path through all of this:

1. Check mute, volume, and audio output setting (TV Speaker) — 60 seconds
2. Check SAP / Secondary Audio mode — 60 seconds
3. Full power cycle with 30-second button hold — 2 minutes
4. Reseat all cables, swap HDMI cable if possible — 5 minutes
5. Switch inputs to isolate the source — 3 minutes
6. Check soundbar/external speaker connection and do joint power cycle — 5 minutes
7. Run the built-in speaker test — 2 minutes
8. Check for and install firmware update — 10 minutes
9. Check app-specific audio format settings if problem is one app only — 5 minutes
10. Factory reset as last software option — 15 minutes
11. If speaker test failed and factory reset didn't help — hardware diagnosis needed

The large majority of TV picture-but-no-sound cases resolve somewhere between steps 1 and 6. Steps 7 through 10 cover the less common but real software-level causes. Step 11 is genuine hardware failure — the least likely explanation and the one most people assume first.
