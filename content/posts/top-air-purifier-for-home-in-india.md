## What You Must Understand Before Buying Any Air Purifier in India

These four things determine whether an air purifier actually works in your home — and they are almost never clearly explained in product listings.

**CADR is the only spec that matters for sizing.** CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate) measures how many cubic metres of clean air a purifier delivers per hour. Match the CADR to your room size. A bigger room needs a higher CADR. The CADR should be high enough to clean the room in about an hour.

The correct calculation: take your room's length × width × height in metres to get the volume, then choose a purifier whose CADR equals or exceeds that number. A 150 sq ft bedroom with 9-foot ceilings has a volume of roughly 125 cubic metres — you need a CADR of at least 125 m³/h. Always buy one size up from the minimum — manufacturer room size claims assume ideal conditions.

**True HEPA vs HEPA-type is not a minor distinction.** Insist on True HEPA or H13. Reject HEPA-type and HEPA-like, which are marketing terms for filters that do not meet the standard. True HEPA H13 filters capture 99.95% of particles 0.3 microns or larger — this includes PM2.5, dust, pollen, pet dander, and most airborne bacteria. HEPA-type filters capture far less. Check the actual filter grade, not just the word "HEPA" on the box.

**Filter cost is the real long-term expense.** Even the best air purifier in India has ongoing costs. Xiaomi (₹3,499) and Honeywell (₹3,999) have cheaper filters. Coway filters (₹8,980-₹11,980) cost more but last longer (1.5-2 years vs 6-12 months). Calculate the 3-year total ownership cost, not just the purchase price.

**Carbon filter matters for Indian homes specifically.** HEPA catches particles — dust, PM2.5, pollen. It does not remove cooking fumes, agarbatti smoke, mosquito coil chemicals, vehicle exhaust gases, or VOCs from paints and cleaning products. Only carbon catches gases and odours. If you live on a busy road, carbon bed depth matters as much as the HEPA rating. Every home in India near traffic, with regular cooking, or using incense needs a purifier with a genuine activated carbon layer — not just a token carbon pre-filter sheet.

## Room Size Guide — Which CADR Do You Actually Need?

Use this simple calculation before buying:

**Room volume** = Length (metres) × Width (metres) × Height (metres)

The purifier CADR should equal or exceed the room volume for one complete air change per hour. For genuine air quality improvement in Indian pollution conditions, aim for 3 to 5 air changes per hour — meaning choose a purifier whose CADR is 3× to 5× your room volume.

## 4 Things That Most Air Purifier Guides Never Tell You

**1. Placement matters as much as the purifier itself.** Place the purifier in the centre of the room or at least 1 to 2 feet away from walls on all sides. A purifier stuffed into a corner only cleans the air near the corner. For bedrooms, the space beside the bed is the most effective placement — the purifier cleans the air you are actually breathing while sleeping.

**2. Closing windows is essential.** Running an air purifier with windows open is like running your AC with the door open — the purifier cleans air as fast as polluted outdoor air replaces it, resulting in no net improvement. Close windows when the purifier is running, especially during cooking or on high-AQI days.

**3. The pre-filter needs regular cleaning even if the main filter does not.** The mesh pre-filter on most purifiers catches large dust, hair, and debris before it reaches the HEPA filter. Cleaning it with a soft brush or vacuum every 2 to 4 weeks extends the life of the expensive HEPA filter significantly. Most buyers skip this entirely and replace the HEPA filter prematurely.

**4. Check whether replacement filters are actually available before buying.** The purifier that stays in use is the one whose filters you can actually buy. Check replacement price and local availability before purchase, not after. Some purifiers sold in India use filters that are difficult to find outside major cities or are frequently out of stock on Amazon.in. Confirm filter availability for your specific model before committing to the purchase.

**How often should I change my air purifier filter in India?** The main cost is the filter, which lasts six to twelve months. In heavily polluted cities like Delhi and Noida during winter, HEPA filters may need replacement every 6 months. In moderately polluted cities like Bengaluru and Pune, 10 to 12 months is realistic. **Is a True HEPA H13 filter necessary?** Insist on True HEPA or H13. Reject HEPA-type and HEPA-like, which are marketing terms for filters that do not meet the standard. H13 is the minimum for genuine PM2.5 removal — anything less cannot reliably capture the fine particles that cause the most harm in Indian urban air.

**Can an air purifier remove cooking smells and agarbatti smoke?** Only if it has a genuine activated carbon filter, not just a token carbon sheet pre-filter. HEPA catches particles. Only carbon catches gases and odours. All six purifiers in this guide include activated carbon, but the Honeywell V5's separate carbon layer handles heavy cooking and VOC environments most effectively.

**What is the right room size for a 300 m³/h CADR purifier?** In real Indian conditions with doors and windows closed, a 300 m³/h CADR purifier provides approximately 3 air changes per hour in a standard 150 sq ft bedroom. This is the minimum for meaningful air quality improvement. For a 300 sq ft bedroom, you need at least 380 to 400 m³/h CADR.
