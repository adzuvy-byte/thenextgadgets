The MacBook Air with the M1 chip launched in November 2020, nearly six years ago. It was Apple's first Apple Silicon laptop, and it is still a capable machine for everyday work. The question in 2026 is whether it is a smart buy, and the honest answer is yes, in the right situation: as a cheap, refurbished laptop for light use. ## The short answer

Buy an M1 Air if you want a light, silent, long-lasting laptop for browsing, documents, Zoom and streaming, and you are buying it at a low price. Skip it if you need multiple monitors, lots of storage or heavy multitasking.

## What still holds up

- **Battery.** Apple rated it up to 15 hours of web browsing and 18 hours of video. Used units may show wear, and reputable sellers typically guarantee at least 85% battery health.
- **Silent and fanless,** with a premium aluminium body.
- **Software support.** The M1 is confirmed for macOS 27, expected in September 2026.
- **Performance.** It can compete with midrange Windows laptops, especially if it has 16 GB of memory. BGR cites tests showing 40 to 55 fps at 1080p in several Mac-ported games with MetalFX Performance mode.

## Limits you should know

- **8 GB base RAM** handles browsing, documents, Zoom and 4K streaming, but many tabs and apps will strain it.
- **Display:** 13.3-inch panel with 400 nits max, which is hard to read in bright sun.
- **Storage:** 256 GB base is limiting, and bigger configurations cost more.
- **Ports:** only two USB-C ports. A hub can add more.
- **Monitors:** supports a single external display without workarounds.

## Who it suits

- Students and budget buyers who want a light laptop for web, documents and calls.
- People who value battery, silence and build quality.
- Anyone buying a first Mac on a tight budget.

## Who should skip it

- Multi-monitor setups and desk workstations.
- Large photo, video or music libraries on a 256 GB drive.
- Outdoor work in bright light.
