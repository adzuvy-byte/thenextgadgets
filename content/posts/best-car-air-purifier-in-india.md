## What a car air purifier can and cannot do

Car purifiers are small. They clean the air in the cabin slowly, so they help most in a closed car with the AC on recirculate. They will not remove all pollution, and they cannot match a home purifier.

- **PM2.5 and dust.** A True HEPA filter is the proper way to trap fine particles. Ionizers clump particles so they settle, which is less effective.
- **Smells and gases.** Activated carbon helps with some odours and gases, but the small amount in a car unit is limited.
- **Ozone.** Some ionizers can release small amounts of ozone, which is a lung irritant. Choose units that state they are ozone-safe, and do not run an ionizer in a closed car with sensitive passengers.
- **Germ claims.** Claims like killing 99.9 percent of viruses and bacteria come from manufacturers and are usually based on lab tests, not real cars. A purifier is not a substitute for ventilation or cleaning.
- **CADR.** A listed CADR of 24 or 25 m³/h is small. For a cabin of roughly 3 m³ it gives only a rough idea of how often the air is cycled, and it is measured under test conditions.
- **Filter claims.** 99.97 percent applies to HEPA filters at a specific particle size under test conditions, not to the whole cabin.

## How to choose

- **True HEPA filter.** Check it is not just "HEPA type".
- **Airflow.** A stronger fan cleans the cabin faster but is louder.
- **Power.** Most run on USB or the 12 V socket. Check your car has a suitable one.
- **Filter replacement.** Check price and availability of replacement filters.
- **Size.** It should fit a cup holder or seat back without blocking your view.
- **Sensor and display.** Helpful, but treat the numbers as indicative.

## Tips to use it well

- Close windows, use recirculate and run it from the start of the journey.
- Replace the filter on schedule, sooner in dusty areas.
- Vacuum the interior regularly. Dust in the car re-enters the air.
